What is this group?

  • Who we are

    An open, informal group of people who care about cognitive accessibility. Anyone can join. You do not need to be an expert.

  • What we do

    We talk about what makes websites and apps hard to understand and use. We write it down and send it to the people who write the official guidance.

  • How we meet

    On Zoom, on the first Thursday of each month at 15:00 UTC. Between meetings we chat on Discord.

  1. You shareAn experience, an example or an idea.
  2. The team leads collectKiki, Cat and Anya put the group's input together.
  3. The Task Force decidesThe W3C COGA Task Force writes the official guidance.

Good to know. Meetings are not recorded. If you share a personal story, you can ask for it to stay in the room.

Your first 3 steps

Do these in any week you like. There is no deadline.

  1. Join the chat

    Join Discord and add the meeting to your calendar. The links are on the Home tab of the notes document.

  2. Read one meeting

    Open '26 Meeting Notes and read the newest meeting. That is enough to follow the next call.

  3. Share one thing

    Vote 👍 for a topic you care about. Or write a few lines about something online that was hard for you. Any format is fine.

1.How the group works

The Community Group is the informal side of COGA. Anyone can join and bring lived experience, research or frustrations. The team leads turn that into structured feedback for the W3C COGA Task Force, which writes the official guidance.

Note

Ground rules: everyone gets a chance to speak. If you share a personal story, say at the start if it must stay in the room. Meetings are not recorded. The transcript and chat are used only to write the notes.

Note

Why a Community Group? It lets people think about possible standards without the strict demands and time commitments of a formal standards body. The group "constructively complains" to the Task Force.

2.2Action | review by 18 Oct '26

Read first Current work, about 10 minutes. Tab: Action | review by 18 Oct '26, sub-tab Separate comments

Summary

An early Task Force draft, User Testing for Cognitive Accessibility: Challenges, Principles, and Practical Solutions for Teams of Every Size, pasted in for the group to comment on. This is the group's main task right now.

Issue 1: deadline unclear

The tab title says 18 October, the October meeting notes say 21 October, and the draft was still on the 12 November agenda. Ask Kiki whether comments are still useful. Add comments directly in the draft or at the bottom of the tab.

What the draft argues

  • Automated testing can't tell if people with cognitive disabilities can actually use something. You have to involve them.
  • The testing process itself must be accessible: recruitment, consent, scheduling, the testing platform.
  • Record frustration, needed help and independence as data, not only task completion.
  • Move from "testing users" to "involving users", using the Easy Reading peer-researcher model.

How the draft is structured

  1. Introduction and current practice
  2. Challenges for users: being left out, consent online, distress
  3. Challenges for developers: cost, diversity, tooling
  4. Problems with current online tools
  5. Principles ("do no harm", "test behaviour, not opinions")
  6. Solutions for user needs
  7. Solutions for developers and a proposal for AG
  8. Closing note, then references

What the community has said so far

  • Anya: restructure around the testing process, cite every claim, show clearly what is different (the "delta") for cognitive disabilities, and explain how it relates to the testing section already in Content Usable.
  • A member suggested covering manipulative patterns and testing of AI chatbots, where risks of addiction and parasocial relationships are higher.
  • Kiki (comment of 9 November): resources are scattered through the draft and should sit with the related content.

2.4'26 Opinion Papers

Read first Start with the Voting Area. Tab: '26 Opinion Papers, with Voting Area, one sub-tab per paper, the template, Idea Generation and Prioritized list

Summary

Opinion papers are 1 to 2 page pieces (500 to 750 words) on one gap that WCAG and Content Usable don't cover: the problem, then what research says, then a real story. Members add a 👍 to show which topics matter. The votes help the Task Force prioritize.

Finished paperVotesAuthorArgument
Endless scrolling [Mobile]7KikiEndless feeds remove signposts and stopping points, which disorients people and takes away their agency. Proposes intentional friction: breaks, "load more", time and progress indicators, alerts for content already seen.
Readability formulas3EricFormulas like Flesch-Kincaid can measure text but shouldn't be used to write it. Writing to the formula produces "caveman speak". Asks for a warning in Understanding SC 3.1.5 and Techniques G86 and G153.
Visual hierarchy (Gestalt)3SimoProximity, similarity and common container should be testable. Items in a group should sit closer together than separate groups (the radio-button example). The same style shouldn't be used for opposite actions.

Drafts and ideas still open

  • AI Content Disclosure (draft): undisclosed AI content forces people to check facts themselves, which taxes working memory, executive function and literacy. Cites the EU AI Act, Article 50.
  • System Utility & Cognitive Agency (draft, AI-generated): extend COGA from usability to whether a system serves the user's real goals.
  • Impact of GenAI chats on cognition (draft, votes: 1 for, 2 against): the illusion of learning, echo chambers, loss of agency.
  • Not ready yet: COGA tips for users, Content alternatives (Anya). To be decided: Legibility.
  • Idea Generation and Prioritized list: where topics started (December 2025, first drafts due 5 February 2026).

2.6'25 Feedback to COGA Task Force

Skim when needed The summary is enough. Sub-tabs: Gaps in COGA 'Content Usable' Guidelines, Good multimodal content examples, Notes, Questions, Style Considerations

Summary

The group's biggest piece of work so far. A ranked list of what Content Usable is missing, presented to Lisa and Rain from the Task Force in February 2025. Many 2026 opinion papers grew out of it.

RankGapThe ask
1Endless scroll and video feedsTime and progress indicators, prompts to take breaks
2Multimodal communicationMake pattern 8.2 easier to read. Account for people who hide images and alt text.
3Gestures (mobile) and intentional frictionClear affordances, a non-gesture alternative, friction before destructive actions
4Comparisons, AI and automationSide-by-side views and the same units. AI may reduce load but must not decide for the user.
5Responsive layoutInformation must not disappear in small windows
6Simple, linear structureMore information architecture, less dispersion
7EmojisClarify pattern 4.4.12. Don't use emojis instead of text.
8MetadataAuthor, date and context, so people can judge whether to trust content
9Data visualizationsWhich chart types are hard and why

Other items: deceptive-pattern protection (no hidden costs, no manipulative language, no forced actions), pre-selected options, onboarding, icons, tricky components, trade-offs.

Good multimodal content examples

A collection of good and bad examples, from blog posts with images grouped by topic to Netflix autoplay as a bad case.

Style Considerations

Comments on the Content Usable redesign: wording ("use", "identify", "content"), sentence case, one glossary, unique identifiers for each guideline.

4.Glossary

COGA
Cognitive and Learning Disabilities Accessibility.
Task Force (TF)
The official W3C group that writes COGA guidance. Co-chairs include Lisa and, since 2026, Julie.
Community Group (CG)
This group: open, informal, gives feedback.
Content Usable
The main COGA guidance document, with objectives, patterns and user stories.
Research Modules
Task Force papers on specific topics, published as a W3C draft note in February 2026.
Functional needs
Describing what a person needs (memory, attention and so on) rather than a diagnosis. This is the WCAG 3 approach.
Opinion paper
A one-page argument about a gap, with research and a real story.
User story
Your experience written in your own words. The leads turn it into formal user needs.
AP
Action point: a task with a named owner.
AG
Accessibility Guidelines Working Group, which develops WCAG.
TPAC and CSUN
The annual W3C conference and a large accessibility conference.

5.Your first month

  1. Join Discord and subscribe to the shared calendar (links on the Home tab).
  2. Read this guide, then the latest sub-tab in '26 Meeting Notes.
  3. Add 👍 votes in the Voting Area for topics that matter to you.
  4. Write one user story about a barrier you hit online. Any format works: text, bullets, voice note.
  5. Optional: fill in an intro slide, or pick an open opinion paper to help with.
